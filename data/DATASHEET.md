# Datasheet: Larkspur demo dataset

Format follows *Datasheets for Datasets* (Gebru et al.).

## Motivation
Built to demonstrate Support Signal. Larkspur is a fictional B2B company selling workforce scheduling software (Rota, Timesheets, Payroll Connect). Every flaw is planted deliberately so each verdict teaches something. Nothing is derived from any real company's data, taxonomy or distributions.

## Composition
- 900 fictional accounts; 875 raised at least one case.
- About 5,100 case rows over twelve months (1 Oct 2025 to 30 Sep 2026).
- Two exports of the same cases: `larkspur_snapshot.csv` (final state only) and `larkspur_with_history.csv` (adds reopen counts, owner and group counts, customer wait time), plus `larkspur_history_log.csv` (owner, group and status changes).
- Two more exports of the same cases in one-row-per-comment shape: `larkspur_comment_rows.csv` and `larkspur_comment_rows_with_duplicates.csv`. See [Comment layer](#comment-layer-one-row-per-case-comment).
- 1,500 AI bot conversations in two matching files: `larkspur_bot_snapshot.csv` (no link to later cases, no reopen counts) and `larkspur_bot_with_history.csv` (adds `reopens` and `linked_case_ids`). See [Bot layer](#bot-layer-for-the-resolution-audit).
- `larkspur_bot_truth.csv` is the answer key for the bot layer. It is not part of either export.
- Customer text is template-generated. Names are placeholders ("Agent K").

## Collection process
Generated by `generator/generate_demo.py` with fixed seed 20261001. Running it twice produces identical files. The bot layer runs after the cases are written, on its own random stream seeded `[20261001, 1]`, so adding it left the case exports, history log, planted flaws and drivers byte-for-byte unchanged.

## Planted flaws and expected verdicts

| Flaw | How it's planted | Expected verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duplicate rows | 0.5% of rows repeated identically | C4 amber in the one-row-per-case exports; green in the comment exports, where the same rows collapse to clean cases |
| History starts late | Field history switched on 12 Jan 2026 | C3 amber (history export) |
| Catch-all reasons | About a quarter of cases logged "Other", "General" or blank | B2 red |
| Near-duplicate reasons | "Login issue" / "Login Issues", "SSO" / "Single sign-on" | B3 amber |
| Bad dates | About 1% of closed cases closed before they were created | B4 amber |
| No wait time in snapshot | Only the history export has customer wait time | B5 needs a human (snapshot), green (history) |
| Unlinked follow-ups | Reopen counts exist but follow-ups aren't linked | B6 amber (history export) |
| Ignored escalation flag | Flag set on a small fraction of cases that went to engineering | B7 needs a human (snapshot), red (history) |
| Team-type reasons | "Tier 2", "Escalated to Engineering", "Billing team" on about 13% of cases | AI2 amber |
| Thin resolution records | Resolution notes or articles on under half of closed cases | AI4 amber |
| Survey response bias | Quickly resolved cases are surveyed more often | Noted in B8 |
| No bot export at all | Case files carry no bot conversations | B11 not in export |
| Bot conversations with no case links | `larkspur_bot_snapshot.csv` has account and timings only | B11 amber, audit all "can't tell" |
| Bot conversations with case links | `larkspur_bot_with_history.csv` adds `linked_case_ids` | B11 green, audit runs |

## The planted causal structure (for the driver panel)
- Churn depends on **product** (Payroll Connect churns most) and on one genuine service effect: an account having a case that was **slow (30+ days) and passed around (3+ owners)**.
- Case volume depends on product and account size, **not** on churn.
- So raw "drivers" correlated with case volume (10+ cases, phone contact, high priority, "Other" reasons) look predictive and fall away once product and case volume are controlled. The planted effect holds.
- With a dozen flags tested, the odd one will look protective by chance. That's left in deliberately.

## Bot layer (for the resolution audit)
The bot conversations are a fictional AI agent's chats, each with a `claimed_resolved` flag. The audit question is what happened after the bot claimed a resolution. There are three answers: **contradicted**, **not contradicted** (silence, which is not the same as success), and **can't tell** (the export doesn't link bot conversations to later cases).

### Columns
| File | Columns |
| --- | --- |
| Both bot files | `bot_conversation_id`, `started_at`, `ended_at`, `account_id`, `account_size`, `product`, `intent` (the bot's detected theme), `customer_message`, `claimed_resolved` |
| With history only | `reopens` (times the conversation was reopened), `linked_case_ids` (every human case the same account opened in the 30 days after `ended_at`, separated by semicolons) |

The bot files are mapped in bot scope, so their headers are matched only against fields tagged `file: bot` or `file: both` in `rules/rules.yaml`. Two consequences worth knowing when reading these columns:

- `reopens` is deliberately a header both scopes claim: in a case export it is the case field `reopen_count`, which check B6 reads, and in a bot export it is `bot_reopens`, which the audit's reopened bucket reads. Scoping is the only thing telling them apart, and `npm test` asserts both directions.
- No column is named `follow_up_of`, `parent_case` or `related_case`, and no bot field claims those headers, so the bot link column can never be read as the case field `follow_up_of`. Scoping decides what a header means per file, though, not which file it is: a bot export handed to the case slot is still read as cases.

### Planted counts
All counts are exact, not sampled. They are recorded in `data/generation_stats.json` under `bot`.

| Planted type | How it's built | Count | Share of claimed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reopened | Claimed resolved, `reopens` 1 or 2, no case in the next 30 days | 72 | 6% |
| Escalated to a human | Claimed resolved, then a Chat case on the same theme 5 to 55 minutes after the bot ended | 96 | 8% |
| Same theme back within 7 days | One case, same account and theme, 0.5 to 6.5 days later | 84 | 7% |
| Same theme back within 7 to 14 days | As above, 7.5 to 13.5 days later | 48 | 4% |
| Same theme back within 14 to 30 days | As above, 14.5 to 29.5 days later | 48 | 4% |
| Unrelated repeat | Cases within 30 days, all on a different theme. **Not** a contradiction | 120 | 10% |
| Genuine silence | No case from the account within 30 days | 732 | 61% |
| *Not claimed: handoff* | Bot didn't claim resolution and handed to a human (Chat case within 55 minutes) | 210 | out of scope |
| *Not claimed: abandoned* | Bot didn't claim resolution and nothing followed | 90 | out of scope |

Cumulative same-theme returns: 84 within 7 days, 132 within 14, 180 within 30.

### Rules that keep every label unambiguous
- The window is (`ended_at`, `ended_at` + 30 days]. "Same theme" means the case `reason` names the bot's `intent`. The near-duplicate spellings "Login Issues" and "Single sign-on" count as their base theme. 16 planted follow-ups use one of these spellings, so the audit must normalise reasons. Those 16 split 10 same-theme returns, which only reason normalisation recovers, and 6 escalations, which timing catches whatever the spelling. Turning `normalise_reasons` off costs exactly the 10.
- A planted follow-up is the **only** case in its window. Unrelated repeats have only cases with readable, different reasons in the window. Silence, reopened and abandoned conversations have no cases in their window.
- Planted follow-ups never carry a catch-all, team-type or rare reason, so the theme is always readable.
- Escalation is told apart from a quick return by timing: under 60 minutes is escalation. The generator then leaves the next 12 hours empty of same-theme follow-ups, so no planted label depends on where the return window starts. The engine's rule has no such gap — returns begin the moment the escalation window ends, because a same-theme case three hours later is a return and banking it as silence would be the one answer it can't be. Closing the gap leaves every planted label and every total unchanged, which is what the empty 12 hours is for. Lags also stay at least 12 hours clear of the 7, 14 and 30-day boundaries.
- Every claimed-resolved conversation ends at least 30 days before the export ends (31 Aug 2026), so no window is cut short. A single account's bot windows never overlap.
- The follow-up cases are existing case rows. Bot conversations were timed to fit them, and no case was added or changed.
- 92 of the 732 silent conversations belong to accounts that churned, so silence is not success.

### Expected audit result
| Export | Contradicted | Not contradicted | Can't tell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Snapshot (`larkspur_bot_snapshot.csv`) | 0 | 0 | 1,200 |
| With history (`larkspur_bot_with_history.csv`) | 348 | 852 | 0 |

The per-conversation answer is in `larkspur_bot_truth.csv` (`planted`, `audit_snapshot`, `audit_with_history`, `follow_up_case_id`, `lag_days`, `account_churned`). After writing, the generator re-derives every label from the exported files alone and stops if any label or count disagrees. The engine never reads this file; only the tests do, and a test asserts the engine opens no files at all.

### What the engine implements, and what it doesn't
B11 green is implemented: where `linked_case_ids` exists, the audit follows it and reproduces all 1,200 planted labels. B11 amber — account and timings, no links — is **not** implemented. Those conversations are reported "can't tell", which is why the snapshot export's 1,200 claimed conversations all land there.

Two things a later step needs to know:

- **This dataset cannot test the amber path.** On these files, matching by account plus the 30-day window reproduces `linked_case_ids` exactly: 1,200 of 1,200 conversations, nothing extra, nothing missed. That follows from how the data is built — `linked_case_ids` *is* every case the account opened in those 30 days, and one account's bot windows never overlap. So a fixture made by dropping the link column would score full marks and prove nothing about the cases that make timing-based matching hard. Testing it needs ambiguity planted on purpose: accounts with two overlapping bot windows, a case that falls inside both, and a case belonging to no conversation, with the truth file recording which conversation each follow-up really belongs to.
- **B11's amber title reads wrong.** The engine titles any non-green check with its `failure_title`, so B11 at amber is headed "Bot conversations aren't linked to later cases" when the point of amber is that they *are* linked, by account and timing. The precise wording is in the check's `detail`, taken verbatim from `outcomes.warn` in `rules/rules.yaml`. B6 has the same quirk at amber. Fixing it means changing how titles are chosen for every check, so it belongs with the amber path rather than ahead of it.

## Comment layer (one row per case comment)

Real helpdesk exports are often one row per case comment or email, with the case fields repeated on every row. `larkspur_comment_rows.csv` is exactly that shape, built from the same cases as `larkspur_snapshot.csv`.

### How it's built
Every comment is derived **deterministically** from the case row it belongs to — a hash of the case ID, no random stream at all. Three consequences the tests depend on:

- The case exports, history log and bot layer are byte-for-byte unchanged. `npm run data` is asserted on this.
- A case row that the duplicate planter repeated expands to an **identical set of comment rows**. They are still identified and dropped, mixed in with a genuine one-to-many shape, which is the case worth testing: a one-to-many export can still contain a row sent twice, and the two must not be confused. They no longer fail C4, because after collapsing the case is still there once — see [C4 by shape](#c4-is-judged-at-case-level-by-shape).
- Comment bodies are built from the case's own **subject**, never its description, plus neutral operational wording that contains no theme, product or billing word. So the comment text introduces nothing the churn flags were not already reading, and the collapsed file can be asserted to give the same verdicts.

### Columns
| Column | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `comment_id` | `LS-10234-C2`, the case ID and the comment's position |
| `comment_created_at` | Spread across the case's life, oldest first |
| `comment_author_id` | The case owner for agent comments, the account ID for customer ones |
| `comment_author_type` | `Agent` or `Customer` |
| `comment_public` | `Yes`, or `No` for an internal note |
| `comment_body` | Neutral wording; the subject leads the first comment |

`last_update_at` is **dropped on purpose**. With it gone the engine has to derive the last update from the latest public comment, which is the path worth testing. One case in four ends on an internal note, so for those the latest public comment is *not* the latest comment, and a derivation that ignored the public flag would be caught.

### Counts
All exact, recorded in `generation_stats.json` under `comments`.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Rows | 15,227 | 15,689 |
| Cases | 5,083 | 5,083 |
| Comments per case | 1 / 3 / 5 (min / median / max) | same |
| Rows identical to an earlier row | 67 | 529 |
| Cases ending on an internal note | 1,321 | same |
| C4 | green | green |
| Cases whose rows disagree | 0 | 0 |

The second file repeats *individual comment rows*, not whole cases: 529 of 15,689, which would have cleared C4's amber band when C4 was measured on rows. It now measures cases, and both files collapse to the same 5,083 cases with nothing disagreeing, so both pass. The file's purpose is unchanged but its point is sharper: the rows are still found, still dropped, still reported in what was read, and still raise a caution on anything read from comment counts or comment text — and none of that is allowed to leak into a verdict about the cases.

### C4 is judged at case level, by shape
A repeated case ID means opposite things in the two shapes, so C4 asks a different question of each.

| Shape | What C4 measures | On these files |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One row per case | Share of rows whose case ID appears more than once | 50 of 5,108 rows, 1.0%, amber |
| One row per comment | Share of cases whose case-level columns disagree between their rows | 0 of 5,083, green |

The shape is decided **after** rows identical in every column are dropped, which is what keeps the two apart: a plain export whose only repetition is identical copies never looks like a comment export, so it is judged the old way and the planted duplicates still show up as amber.

Rows identical in every column are dropped in both shapes. In a one-row-per-case export they are also a repeated case ID, so they count towards C4. In a comment export they do not. They are instead:

- **reported** in "What was read", with the likely cause named: an export carrying no comment ID or timestamp makes two short replies sent in the same minute, "Thanks" twice, look like one row sent twice;
- **a caution**, not a block, on anything measured from comment counts or comment text. Today that is AI1 and the "any billing or invoice case" churn flag, both of which read comment bodies. A caution never changes an outcome, a state or a lock, and signals that read only reasons and resolution times are left alone.

Cautions are declared in `rules/rules.yaml`: a check or signal says `reads: [comment_text]` and the caution says which `reads` tags it applies to, so a future reader of comment counts inherits it by saying so rather than by being named in the engine.

### Expected verdicts
Every check, use, signal and churn driver on the collapsed `larkspur_comment_rows.csv` matches `larkspur_snapshot.csv` — with one deliberate exception, **C4**, which is amber on the snapshot and green here. The same 25 repeated case rows are two rows for one case there and the file's shape here. The exception is listed in `expected.json` under `comments_differ_from_snapshot`, and a test asserts each listed check really does differ, so an exception can't be used to paper over a drift. Everything else is asserted equal to the snapshot's block rather than listed twice.

Two details change without changing a verdict:

- C4's `detail` names the shape, the number of cases spanning more than one row, and whether the collapse was clean.
- AI1's `detail` reports how many cases have usable wording *only* in the comment text. The share can only rise, never fall, so the outcome stays green.

### What this layer does not test
Nothing here exercises a **case-level conflict** — the case fields are repeated faithfully on every row, so there is nothing to disagree. That case is covered by a hand-built fixture in `test/engine.test.mjs` which mixes all three causes of a repeated case ID in nine rows: a one-to-many case, a redundant copy, a case whose owner disagrees between rows, and a case whose repeated fields are blanked rather than repeated (which is not disagreement, and takes the first real value).

## Uses
Demonstration and testing of Support Signal only. Not suitable for benchmarking real support operations.

## Maintenance
Regenerate with `npm run data` (needs numpy). Expected verdicts live in `data/expected.json` and are enforced by `npm test`. Planted counts live in `data/generation_stats.json`.
